# Open research questions

Organized October 12, 2026. A research queue, not a prescribed program or automatic monitoring schedule.

1. **Weekly hard-set dose:** reconcile low-volume approaches with volume-response evidence, training status, recovery and practical time constraints. Do not count warm-ups or activities as hard sets without checking definitions.
2. **Effort targets:** clarify which populations and training doses benefit from failure versus near-failure. [The failure review](../evidence/2026-10-05-failure-rir.md) does not settle the optimal individual RIR.
3. **Time efficiency:** determine which antagonist pairings and rest structures preserve useful training quality in different contexts. Prioritize actual time constraints and measured response over universal rest ceilings.
4. **Low-volume HIIT:** check population, interval/recovery definitions, warm-up/cool-down and uncertainty before translating modeled dose estimates into a protocol.
5. **Specialized methods:** prioritize long-muscle-length work, partial range of motion, advanced set techniques or blood-flow restriction when the evidence would change a concrete decision.
6. **Rest-timing preprint:** revisit the July 2026 preprint when a corrected or peer-reviewed version is available. [The current review](../evidence/2026-10-12-rest-recovery-time-efficiency.md) records important limitations.
7. **Mobility and yoga:** distinguish passive flexibility, active control, task preparation and enjoyment/relaxation; avoid equating them with proven post-exercise recovery benefits.
8. **Adherence and sustainability:** determine which behavior-support strategies produce durable resistance-training participation, and in whom. Distinguish completed training from app engagement or short-term attendance; start with the historical notes on [early consistency](../research/2026-09-16-early-consistency-long-term-adherence.md), [adherence interventions](../research/2026-09-17-resistance-training-adherence-interventions.md) and [mHealth delivery](../research/2026-09-15-mhealth-resistance-training-evidence.md).
9. **Zone 2 definition and dose:** clarify how threshold-relative prescription compares with percentage-based anchors, which field methods are practical without laboratory testing, and what dose adds value alongside VO2-focused work. The [historical intensity note](../research/2026-06-14-zone-2-intensity-prescription.md) is not a source-reviewed prescription.
10. **Concurrent-training sequencing and modality:** identify when session order, separation and endurance modality materially affect hypertrophy, strength, explosive performance or adherence. Reconcile the historical notes on [interference](../research/2026-05-03-concurrent-training-interference.md), [sequencing](../research/2026-05-21-concurrent-training-sequencing.md) and [modality](../research/2026-06-25-concurrent-training-modality.md) before proposing a fixed rule.
11. **Beginner calisthenics progression:** determine useful progressions and dose for skill acquisition, strength and graded wrist/shoulder exposure without assuming that every bodyweight variation is equivalent resistance work. Start with the historical notes on [beginner integration](../research/2026-05-06-beginner-calisthenics-integration.md), [scapular preparation](../research/2026-06-16-scapular-control-calisthenics-prep.md), [wrist loading](../research/2026-06-30-wrist-loading-calisthenics-prep.md) and [push-up progression](../research/2026-07-19-progressive-push-up-calisthenics.md).

New questions should identify the decision they inform, relevant existing evidence and what would resolve the uncertainty.
