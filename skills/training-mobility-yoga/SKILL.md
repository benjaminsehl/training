# Mobility, stretching and yoga coaching

Match the practice to its job: today's movement preparation, longer-term usable range of motion (ROM), or optional yoga/relaxation. Preserve the user's stated priorities; for a hypertrophy-first plan, keep mobility work proportionate to that goal. More flexibility is not automatically better.

For individualized work, read [context and evidence](../references/context-and-evidence.md). For doses, claims, caveats or citations, read the relevant section of [the dated evidence check](references/evidence-2026-10-12.md). It distinguishes checked abstracts, full-text findings, consensus and coaching inference. Refresh evidence for consequential new claims rather than treating the review date as permanent currency.

## Choose the intervention

- **Prepare to lift:** use brief comfortable active/dynamic movements relevant to the upcoming exercise, then progressive task-specific warm-up sets. If a short static stretch improves a needed position, use it selectively and rehearse the movement afterward. Longer cumulative static holds immediately before maximal/forceful work can reduce acute performance; around 60 seconds per muscle is a research grouping, not a precise biological cutoff. Avoid unnecessary warm-up fatigue.
- **Develop ROM:** identify a specific movement limitation and a repeatable outcome. Resistance training through tolerable, controlled ranges can improve ROM while serving strength goals. Add targeted static stretching when useful; do not diagnose the limiting tissue from a sensation of tightness. Distinguish passive flexibility from active control under load.
- **Dose flexibility:** a modest starting option is two comfortable 20–30-second holds per selected target, two or three days weekly, after lifting or separately. Adapt to response. The reported ten-minute weekly plateau is a pooled estimate for targeted exposure, not a minimum or total-body quota. Lower doses can work. Progress range, duration or loading gradually, not all together; avoid forced end ranges.
- **Choose yoga:** ask whether the aim is enjoyment, movement practice or relaxation, and match style/intensity and experience. Gentle practice is optional; demanding flows and loaded end-range holds add training stress. Beginners need accessible modifications; hot yoga and advanced positions are not recovery defaults. Plausible stress/wellness benefits do not establish faster muscular recovery or replacement for lifting/conditioning.

## Return a usable recommendation

State the goal, selected movements, dose, placement, expected benefit and one reassessment criterion. Mark generic examples as provisional; verify current symptoms, schedule and equipment before integrating a personalized plan. Recheck a consistent task after a few weeks; reduce or change work that worsens symptoms or compromises lifting. If ROM is already sufficient, no additional stretching is a valid option.

Do not promise DOMS reduction, blanket injury prevention, posture correction or a pain cure. Radiating pain, numbness, weakness or worsening persistent symptoms require stopping provoking work and appropriate clinical assessment; do not prescribe stretching through them. These instructions authorize no external training-record or program changes.
