# Design a training session

Read [context and evidence](../references/context-and-evidence.md). Establish today's location, equipment, time, current prescription, recent comparable work and readiness. Output either a current-plan adaptation or an explicitly provisional draft.

1. Identify the session's priority stimulus and progression-critical exercises. Preserve those before filling accessories.
2. Select exercises by target, movement, setup, tolerable range and measurable loading. A shared muscle label alone does not make substitutions equivalent; use [session decisions](references/session-decisions.md).
3. Separate preparatory sets from work sets. Specify exercise order, work sets, rep target, effort, rest/readiness criterion, substitution and estimated time. Numerical choices must come from current prescription or be labeled individualized proposals.
4. Budget setup, warm-up and transitions. If time is tight, trim lower-priority work or cautiously pair compatible accessories before rushing priority lifts. Consult the [rest review](../../evidence/2026-10-12-rest-recovery-time-efficiency.md).
5. Define what to record and when to stop or modify. Judge the draft by clean, comparable performance and tolerability, not soreness or discomfort.

Finish with the workout, material differences from the current plan, assumptions, and the next-session comparison needed. Drafting does not update training-system.
