# Source checks for initial coaching skills

Checked 2026-10-12. The review depth is stated for each source. They do not establish an individual's current program or readiness. Findings are intentionally concise; consult originals for a consequential new prescription.

## Weekly volume and frequency

Pelland et al., *The Resistance Training Dose Response*, published December 4, 2025, Sports Medicine 56 (2026), 481–505. [Publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w). Abstract checked; subscription full methods not audited. Meta-regressions: 67 studies, 2,058 participants, mostly young men. Greater volume was associated with gains with diminishing returns. Frequency's hypertrophy effect was compatible with negligible effects; its strength relationship was more consistently positive. Direct versus indirect set accounting influenced models. This supports distinguishing goals and documenting set accounting, not a universal optimal set total, mandatory split, or literal half-set physiology. Split choice below is a scheduling interpretation.

## Autoregulation

Hickmott et al., 2022, [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-021-00404-9). Methods/results checked. Eighteen studies reviewed; fifteen meta-analyzed. Load-prescription strength comparison used six studies/133 participants, mostly young trained males, over 6–12 weeks. Autoregulated versus standardized loads had an uncertain small difference in strength (mean difference 2.07 kg, 95% CI −0.32 to 4.46). Only one load-prescription study measured hypertrophy, precluding that meta-analysis. All studies had some risk of bias. This supports using effort/readiness to choose workable loads without claiming autoregulation or a particular double-progression threshold uniquely maximizes growth. Do not import velocity-loss cutoffs into RIR decisions.

## Concurrent training

Schumann et al., 2022, [PubMed study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/) and [author-institution record](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/71546/). Abstract retrieved through indexed PubMed content; PMC page initially blocked. Forty-three studies of healthy adults doing supervised training. Average effects showed little evidence of compromised whole-muscle hypertrophy or maximal strength; explosive strength was more vulnerable. Protocol heterogeneity and subgroup limitations prevent guarantees for a particular dose or athlete. Avoid blanket claims that all cardio destroys gains or that all combinations are harmless. Scheduling hard endurance away from priority lifting is a practical fatigue-management option, not a universal fixed-hour rule.

## HIIT duration model

Mattioni Maturana et al., *Optimising Exercise Prescription*, published September 12, 2026. [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-026-02528-y). Methods, results and limitations checked. Sixty-nine trials/2,387 participants, predominantly sedentary/low-fitness samples. Modeled 80% of maximal observed VO2 response at 11.4 minutes of work plus recovery; warm-up/cool-down excluded. A work-only sensitivity model estimated 5.2 minutes (95% CI 4.5–7.5). Work:rest varied widely; the authors sometimes added an unreported final recovery to standardize dose. Duration/intensity/protocol differences and underrepresentation of well-trained people limit individual inference. This supports an approximate duration model, not an exact interval protocol or all-in session time. No new personal interval prescription follows.

## Not promoted to verified rules

Exact deload schedules/reductions; fixed weekly muscle-set targets; universal progression percentages; maintenance dose guarantees; advanced intensity methods; lengthened partial prescriptions; device-specific eccentric/resistance curves; BFR; medical exercise treatment. Historical source summaries are not automatically verified by their inclusion in a collection. Verify relevant original sources and individual context before using these claims consequentially.
