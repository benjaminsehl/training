# Hybrid and recovery decisions

## Preserve the chosen priority

Ask the user to rank their goals. A hypertrophy-first configuration can place strength and conditioning around recoverable muscle-building work; an endurance-first configuration may reverse that ordering. An old schedule or preferred device does not establish current capacity or availability.

[Reviewed concurrent-training evidence](../../references/source-checks-2026-10-12.md) supports compatibility on average while distinguishing muscle growth/maximal strength from explosive performance. It does not guarantee that a particular leg session and hard interval dose coexist without fatigue. Avoid both “cardio kills gains” and “interference never matters.”

## Placement choices

- If lifting quality is the priority, protect its highest-value work and examine whether hard conditioning compromises it or the next exposure. Separate sessions or move hard conditioning away from demanding lower-body work when feasible; this is a coaching option, not a validated universal spacing rule.
- If the only available time is after lifting, compare a lighter familiar session, a shorter approved interval dose, or moving/omitting optional conditioning. Do not automatically convert a recoverability goal into all-out sprints.
- If sport/endurance performance is now primary, reassess which session deserves freshness instead of retaining the physique-first ordering silently.
- Consider modality-specific skill, impact, local fatigue, enjoyment and access. No device is universally recovery-free. Current location notes outrank historical ownership.

## Prescribing intensity and dose

State the intensity anchor and its limitations: a known personal threshold, a previously successful protocol, or a clearly described perceived-effort approach. Do not equate every app's “Zone 2,” a guessed age-predicted heart rate, and a measured physiological threshold. Avoid exact watts/heart rates without valid personal data.

The [new HIIT source check](../../references/source-checks-2026-10-12.md) verifies the archived duration model but does not supply a personal workout. Eleven minutes is not an all-in appointment or permission to invent work:rest ratios. Before proposing exact intervals, obtain modality, recent interval experience, recovery tolerance and available total time. Use an established tolerated dose when verified; otherwise make any trial conservative, explicit and conditional. Progress one measurable component at a time, with a plan to reduce it if priority performance or tolerability suffers. Do not promise a particular VO2 increase.

## Recoverability review

Read actual comparable lifting and conditioning logs plus user-reported sleep, soreness, stress, motivation and symptoms. Determine whether a problem is persistent, context-specific or simply missing data. A new hard session, unfamiliar modality, lifting-volume increase, travel or illness can change what is tolerable.

When a repeated problem is demonstrated, identify the plausible competing demand and propose a bounded adjustment; reassess rather than permanently banning a modality. A lower-stress week may reduce volume, effort or difficult scheduling while preserving familiar practice, but fixed day counts or percentage reductions require their own justification. Precise deload protocols remain an evidence-review gap in this release.

Do not diagnose overtraining from fatigue alone, prescribe exercise as blood-pressure treatment, or use unverified bracing/isometric claims for a medical condition. Stop performance optimization when concerning symptoms or restrictions require assessment. For ordinary stretching/yoga choices, use the [separate skill](../../training-mobility-yoga/SKILL.md); flexibility gains and feeling relaxed should not be sold as guaranteed recovery or hypertrophy benefits.

## Record for next review

Modality and setup; work/recovery duration and intensity anchor; total session time; perceived effort and tolerability; subsequent comparable lifting performance. Note why the session was shortened or changed. Missing records do not prove the session was skipped, and perceived difficulty alone does not prove physiological interference.
