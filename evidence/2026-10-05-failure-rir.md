# Is routine failure needed in multi-set training?

Reviewed: 2026-10-05. General evidence synthesis; repository publication prepared October 12. This is a research summary, not an individualized program prescription.

## Verified evidence

### New direct upper-body trial
Vasconcelos et al., *Elbow Flexor Adaptations to Failure Versus Repetitions-in-Reserve Training*, Muscles, published August 30, 2026. [Publisher](https://www.mdpi.com/2813-0413/5/3/61), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/muscles5030061). Publisher indexed text and author-uploaded full paper checked; live publisher fetch returned 429. [Author full-text copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/413782971_Elbow_Flexor_Adaptations_to_Failure_Versus_Repetitions-in-Reserve_Training_A_Unilateral_Within-Participant_Study_in_Resistance-Trained_Adults).

Nineteen trained adults completed eight weeks of unilateral preacher curls, twice weekly, at approximately 70–80% 1RM. One arm trained to failure, the other targeted 2 RIR within 1–3. Sets progressed from five to six per limb/session. Muscle-thickness difference favored failure by 0.032 cm, with 95% HDI −0.044 to 0.113 cm: a small, uncertain difference, not proof of identical outcomes. Strength also improved under both protocols. Randomized limb allocation, supervised training, and blinded outcome assessment help internal validity. Small sample, short duration, one exercise, subjective RIR and possible cross-limb effects constrain generalization. Directly relevant to the idea that isolation work must always reach failure; not a trial of specific cable resistance curves, eccentric overload, or a whole-body routine.

### Counterweight: genuinely low-volume training
Hermann et al., *Without Fail*, 2025 randomized trial: [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40249908/), [author institution abstract/manuscript record](https://rgu-repository.worktribe.com/output/2788957/without-fail-muscular-adaptations-in-single-set-resistance-training-performed-to-failure-or-with-repetitions-in-reserve). Abstract checked; full methods not independently audited here. Forty-two trained men and women were assigned to failure or 2 RIR, one set of nine exercises twice weekly for eight weeks. Several hypertrophy measures modestly favored failure; strength/endurance were similar. This leaves room for tactical failure when set volume is very constrained. It does not establish that failure replaces multiple sets or that all sets in a multi-set program should reach failure. Short study and outcome-specific uncertainty limit confidence.

### Synthesis and professional guidance
Robinson et al., 2024 exploratory meta-regressions: [author institution record](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2). Source abstract verified: closer-to-failure estimates correlated with greater hypertrophy, while strength showed a negligible relationship. RIR was retrospectively estimated; model fit was modest. This supports hard sets but cannot precisely settle 0 versus 1–2 RIR or prove causation.

[ACSM official 2026 guideline summary](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/) checked directly: momentary failure did not consistently improve outcomes for average healthy adults. This broad professional synthesis is useful context, not proof of an optimum for trained lifters. Neither summary nor meta-regression should outweigh specific program context automatically.

## Interpretation and decision

Moderate confidence: repeated true failure is unnecessary as a blanket requirement. Lower confidence: the exact best RIR for each muscle, device setting, and individual. Apparent conflict between near-failure meta-regression, single-set failure benefits, and multi-set RIR trials is partly explained by different comparisons and training doses.

No universal prescription change follows from this review. A 0–1 RIR accessory range permits stopping with one clean repetition left; it need not mean compulsory failure. Allowing more reserve on earlier sets may be worth testing when fatigue is a demonstrated bottleneck, but requires individual context. Technical-max tests should have clear technique stop criteria.

Individual decisions need actual work-set load, repetitions, explicit RIR and recovery across repeated exposures. Do not infer fatigue, stalled progression or volume tolerance from incomplete logs. This review addresses failure as a requirement; it does not settle optimal weekly volume.
