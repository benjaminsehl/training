# Contributing

## Add useful research

1. Search existing summaries and reviews to avoid duplicating the same study.
2. Choose a concrete question and the decision it could inform.
3. Copy [the template](templates/evidence-review.md) to `evidence/YYYY-MM-DD-short-question.md`.
4. Link exact primary sources and record what you actually checked: abstract, full paper, registry, guideline or preprint.
5. Record design, population, sample size, intervention, comparator, duration, outcomes, effect estimates and uncertainty where available.
6. Separate findings, limitations, interpretation and proposed decisions. Explain what changes relative to earlier evidence.
7. Update the evidence index and relevant open questions. Use a descriptive commit message or pull request.

Use GitHub's web editor or a local text editor. No scripts or packages are required.

## Improve a coaching skill

Keep each `SKILL.md` focused on when to use the skill, required context, decisions and a usable output. Put long evidence notes in linked references. Prefer conditional rules and explicit tradeoffs over universal prescriptions.

Test changes against realistic scenarios: missing current context, noisy or incomplete logs, conflicting goals, stalled progression and safety concerns. A skill should not turn a research option into an external program change without authorization. Resolve local Markdown links before publishing.

## Evidence standards

- Clearly label historical summaries, verified source content, interpretation and unresolved questions.
- Do not upgrade a paper's status from abstract-only to fully reviewed without checking it.
- Treat a preprint as provisional and record its version/date.
- Do not silently overwrite a conclusion; explain and link the superseding evidence.
- Cite underlying studies rather than treating repeated summaries as independent support.
- Do not copy full papers, book extracts or proprietary programs. Use your own concise synthesis and link the original.

## Public-repository checklist

- No personal workout/health records, private coaching snapshots, account profiles or unrelated personal information
- No credentials, authentication configuration, secrets or private filesystem links
- No assumptions about a particular reader's goals, equipment, restrictions or active plan
- No unlicensed third-party source reproductions
- Correct dates, functioning relative links, and claims no stronger than their sources

A public research contribution does not authorize collecting personal data or altering someone else's training record.
